# The Slow Money Handbook

This handbook explains how to use the Slow Money series: where to start, how to run the practices, and the few rules that keep them safe and useful. Read the [Manifesto](00-manifesto.md) first for the why. This is the how.

## How the series is built

The series has four parts. Each has a different job.

| Part | What it does | Who it is for |
|---|---|---|
| The spine (Manifesto, FAQ, Handbook, Glossary) | Explains the ideas and shares a common language | Everyone |
| Age tracks (`ages/`) | Teaches what each age needs to understand about money | Children, teenagers, adults, elders |
| Practices (`practices/`) | Brings the generations together around a shared activity | Families and mixed-age groups |
| Tools (`tools/`) | Measures and supports learning, such as the literacy test | Individuals and households |

The age tracks teach. The practices are where the ages meet. Every age track ends by pointing to the practices where that age has a part to play, because Slow Money is learned across generations, not within them.

## Where to start

Start from who you are.

- **A parent or carer:** read the adults track, then try [The Kitchen Table](practices/kitchen-table.md).
- **A grandparent or elder:** read the elders track, then try [Money Memories](practices/money-memories.md), where the younger generation asks and you answer.
- **A teenager:** read the teenagers track. You can start a practice yourself; you don't need to wait for an adult.
- **A child:** the children's track is written to be read with an older family member.
- **A teacher or group organiser:** read the section on using the series as a group, below.

There is no required order. A family that only ever does one practice, once, has still begun.

## Using it as a family

Slow Money has no timetable. The practices work best when they become a habit, but the habit is yours to set.

Some families hold a short money conversation every week, alongside the weekly shop. Others prefer a longer one a few times a year, at a natural turning point: the new year, a birthday, the end of the harvest or the school year. Some keep it for moments that matter: a first job, a move, an inheritance.

Whatever the rhythm, a good first conversation has no numbers in it. Begin with memories and habits. Figures can come later, if the family wants them.

## Ground rules

These apply to every practice in the series.

1. **Nobody has to share numbers.** Earnings, debts and savings are shared only by choice.
2. **Gifts stay gifts.** Care, time and help given freely inside a family are not turned into debts. The practices record decisions and reasons, not who owes whom.
3. **Every age speaks.** The youngest person present may ask any question. The oldest person present is listened to first when the subject is the past.
4. **What is said at the table stays with the family.** Nothing shared in a practice is repeated elsewhere without permission.
5. **No advice, no selling.** Slow Money never recommends a product, an investment or a type of money. Anyone who uses the practices to sell something has left Slow Money.
6. **Stop when it stops being kind.** A conversation that turns into blame can wait for another day. Some matters need outside help, such as a mediator or counsellor, before they can be talked through at home.

## Using it as a group

The series can also be used by schools, community groups and local circles. The same materials work, with a few changes.

- **Host, don't teach.** The organiser's role is to set up the conversation and keep the ground rules, not to deliver answers.
- **Mix the ages.** Groups work best when generations sit together. A room of teenagers alone misses the point of the series.
- **Never collect personal financial information.** A group can discuss how money is handled in families in general. It should not record anyone's actual figures.
- **Send the conversation home.** The aim of a group session is to start conversations that continue at each family's own table.

## Adapting the series

Every family and every place is different. The series is meant to be adapted: translated, rewritten with local examples, extended with local stories of what money meant in that town or valley.

The materials are kept in a public repository so they can be copied and adapted. A local group can keep its own version, with its own translations and stories, while staying connected to the original.

## What Slow Money is not

It is not financial advice, a savings scheme, an investment club or a course that ends with a certificate. It does not ask families to adopt any particular kind of money. It asks them to understand the money they already live with, together, and to choose on purpose what they pass on.
