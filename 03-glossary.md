# Slow Money Glossary

Plain-language meanings of the words used across the Slow Money series. Where a word has a special meaning in Slow Money, that meaning is given first.

**Budget**
A plan for how money coming in will be shared out across what needs to be paid.

**Chronicle**
The family's permanent record, written by hand and kept for good. It records decisions and the reasons for them, never sums or grievances. See [The Family Ledger](practices/family-ledger.md).

**Combined wage**
Everything that comes into a household in a week or a year, added together. Also the name of the practice for looking at it. See [The Combined Wage](practices/combined-wage.md).

**Compound interest**
Interest paid on interest. Over time it makes savings grow faster, and unpaid debts grow faster too.

**Debt**
Money borrowed that must be paid back, usually with interest. Debt takes money from your future self to spend in the present.

**Earning**
Getting money in return for work or a task. Compare *receiving*.

**Economy**
From the Greek *oikonomia*, the management of a household. In Slow Money, every family is an economy, whether or not it thinks of itself as one.

**Family**
Whoever shares a household economy: parents and children, a single parent, grandparents raising grandchildren, siblings sharing a home, or a chosen family.

**Good, clean and fair**
The three values of Slow Food, applied in Slow Money to the money a family lives by. *Good* money serves family life. *Clean* money is free of hidden costs. *Fair* money is honest across generations.

**Heirloom**
Something handed down through a family on purpose. In Slow Money, an heirloom can be a thing, a piece of knowledge, or a mindset. See [The Heirloom](practices/heirloom.md).

**Hidden ledger**
The unspoken accounts a family carries: loans never repaid, help never acknowledged, promises about the house. Never written down, and often the cause of quarrels at inheritance.

**Hidden wage**
What comes into a household without being paid for: food grown at home, childcare from grandparents, repairs done by a relative. Real income, even though it never appears on a payslip.

**Inflation**
The general rise in prices over time. It means the same amount of money buys a little less each year.

**Interest**
The price of borrowing money. A borrower pays it; a saver may be paid it.

**Kitchen Table**
The core Slow Money practice: a family conversation about money, held around a shared meal, with every generation present. See [The Kitchen Table](practices/kitchen-table.md).

**Ledger**
A record of what is owed and paid. In Slow Money, a family decides which of its exchanges belong in a ledger and which do not.

**Money memories**
What the older generation remembers about money. Also the name of the interview practice for collecting them. See [Money Memories](practices/money-memories.md).

**Mindset**
The habits and attitudes a person holds about money, often learned in childhood without being taught. Whether money is spoken about or hidden, feared or trusted, spent at once or kept.

**Needs and wants**
A *need* is something we cannot do without, such as food or a home. A *want* is something we would like but can live without.

**Payslip**
The note that comes with a wage, showing what was earned and what was taken off for tax and other contributions.

**Receiving**
Getting money as a gift, such as a present or pocket money. Compare *earning*.

**Release**
In the Family Ledger, the act of saying aloud that something once owed is now a gift. Once released, it is never raised again.

**Saving**
Keeping money for later instead of spending it now.

**Slate**
In the Family Ledger, the visible, temporary record of real obligations between family members. Each item is wiped clean when it is settled.

**Slow Money**
A way of thinking about money that starts with the family rather than the individual, treats financial understanding as something passed between generations, and prefers the long view to the quick return. See the [Manifesto](00-manifesto.md).

**The table**
In the Family Ledger, everything a family gives freely: care, time, meals, help. Never recorded, and never counted as a debt.

**Time preference**
How much a person values having something now rather than later. People with a low time preference find it easier to wait, save and plan ahead.

**Whose promise is it?**
The question to ask about any form of money: who, if anyone, must you trust for it to still be there next year? Cash, bank balances, app balances, gold, land and Bitcoin each give a different answer.
